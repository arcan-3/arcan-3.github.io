Four years underwater

Bremen UAS · TUHH smartPORT · Helmut Schmidt University · 2014–2018

On a CV these are three separate entries at three institutions. They were one subject approached from three directions: characterise the channel, transmit through it, then pull a specific source back out of it.

The medium is the difficulty

A water column is a hostile channel. Sound travels slowly enough that platform motion produces significant Doppler. Sound speed varies with depth, temperature and salinity, so rays bend. The surface and the seabed reflect, so every transmission arrives several times with different delays and amplitudes. Ambient noise is non-stationary and often biological. Almost everything that makes RF communication tractable is absent.

Characterising the channel — Bremen, 2014–2015

A project on channel state estimation in a water column, under Prof. Dr. Dieter Kraus. The question is the one that underlies everything else: given what arrives at the receiver, what can be said about the path it took? Estimate the channel, and the rest becomes tractable. Fail to, and nothing downstream is reliable.

Transmitting through it — smartPORT, TUHH, 2016–2017

Chirp Modulation for Resilient Underwater Acoustic Communication, supervised by Prof. Bernd-Christian Renner. I built a signal generation and evaluation framework for underwater acoustic communication experiments and compared chirp modulation against frequency shift keying.

Chirps spread a symbol across a band of frequencies over time. That makes them tolerant of the frequency-selective fading a multipath channel produces, and it makes correlation detection sharp. The measured result was a higher data rate together with better Doppler resilience than FSK — the second part mattering more, because Doppler is the thing you cannot design around when the platform is moving.

Pulling a source back out of it — passive sonar, 2017–2018

My master's thesis, Source Separation and Echo Analysis of Underwater Acoustic Signals of Whales, supervised by Prof. Udo Zölzer and Carlos de Obaldía, at Helmut Schmidt University. Passive sonar: no transmitter, only what arrives.

  • Wiener-filter-based source separation to isolate whale signals from the surrounding acoustic field.
  • Three qualitative features for click-based classification of toothed whales — the classification problem being what a separation method is ultimately for.
  • Correlator-based analysis improved by mitigating phase-offset effects in the received signal, which is where multipath quietly ruins detection.

Everything I have done since is a version of this. Change the medium and the instrument, and the question stays: what is really there, given a measurement that only partly shows it?

Where it went next

In 2025 I worked on Wi-Fi Channel State Information for passive environmental sensing — estimating an RF channel and reading motion and presence out of how it changes. It is the Bremen problem in a different medium, eleven years later. The propagation physics differs; the estimation problem does not.
